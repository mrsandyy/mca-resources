# UNIT-2: Functional Grammar

This unit focuses on the structural rules of the English language, including parts of speech, sentence construction, common errors, and vocabulary enhancement.

## 2.1 Different Types of Parts of Speech (POS)

Grammar analyzes language at the level of words. Every word belongs to a specific class called a "Part of Speech" based on its function within a sentence.

### 1. Nouns

- **Function:** Refer to people, places, things, ideas, or qualities.

- **Key Features:**
  
  - Can be modified by adjectives.
  
  - Often preceded by articles (*a, an, the*).
  
  - Act as the **Subject** or **Object** of a clause.
  
  - Categorized as **Countable** (e.g., *apple*) or **Uncountable** (e.g., *water*).

### 2. Pronouns

- **Function:** Words that "stand for" or refer back to a noun or noun phrase to avoid repetition.

- **Key Features:**
  
  - **Objective Case:** Must be used when the pronoun is an object (e.g., *him*, *me*, not *he*, *I*).
  
  - **Agreement:** Must agree in gender and number with the noun they replace (e.g., *The team finished **its** project*).

### 3. Adjectives

- **Function:** Describe or modify nouns/pronouns, providing details about qualities or attributes.

- **Key Features:**
  
  - **Placement:** Usually come before the noun (*a **red** car*) or after linking verbs (*The car is **red***).
  
  - **Linking Verbs:** Used after verbs like *be, seem, become, appear, feel* where they modify the subject, not the verb.

### 4. Verbs

- **Function:** Words referring to actions, states, or conditions.

- **Key Features:**
  
  - **Essentiality:** Every clause *must* contain a verb phrase.
  
  - **Agreement:** Must agree with the subject in number and person (e.g., *She **is** / We **are***).
  
  - **Types:**
    
    - **Full/Main Verbs:** Carry the meaning (e.g., *run, eat*).
    
    - **Auxiliary Verbs:** Help form tenses or moods (e.g., *be, do, have, can, may*).

### 5. Adverbs

- **Function:** Modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. They answer *how, when, where,* or *to what extent*.

- **Types:** Time, Place, Manner, Degree, Frequency.

- **Placement:** Flexible; can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of a clause.

### 6. Connectives / Conjunctions

- **Function:** Joining words used to combine clauses.

- **Types:**
  
  - **Coordinating:** Join equal parts (e.g., *and, but, or*).
  
  - **Subordinating:** Introduce dependent clauses (e.g., *when, although, because*).

### 7. Prepositions

- **Function:** Link a noun/pronoun to another word, usually indicating relationship in time or space.

- **Key Features:**
  
  - Always followed by an object (noun/pronoun).
  
  - The pronoun following a preposition must be in the **objective case** (e.g., *with **him***, not *with he*).

## 2.2 Sentence Structure and Par Jumbles

### A. Sentence Structure (Syntax)

Syntax refers to the rules for arranging words to form coherent sentences.

#### 1. The 5 Functional Elements of a Sentence

A simple sentence is constructed using these five elements:

1. **Subject (S):** What the clause is about. Usually comes before the verb (Noun/Pronoun).

2. **Verb (V):** The central action or state.

3. **Object (O):** The entity affected by the action. Usually comes after the verb.

4. **Complement (C):** Provides more info about the Subject (after linking verbs) or Object.

5. **Adverbial (A):** Provides context (when, where, how, why).

#### 2. Types of Sentences

- **Simple Sentences:** Contain **one** finite clause (one main verb).

- **Compound Sentences:** Contain two or more independent clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions (*and, but, or*).

- **Complex Sentences:** Contain one main clause and one or more **subordinate (dependent)** clauses.
  
  - *Subordinate clauses often function as Adverbials (time/reason) or Nouns.*

### B. Par Jumbles (Jumbled Sentences)

The goal is to rearrange scrambled parts of a sentence or paragraph into a logical order.

- **Strategy:** Look for connecting words (conjunctions), pronouns pointing back to antecedents, and chronological markers to establish relationships between parts.

## 2.3 Error Correction

This section focuses on common grammatical mistakes, particularly those made by non-native speakers.

### 1. Subject-Verb Agreement (Concord)

- **Rule:** The verb must agree with the subject in number.

- **Special Cases:**
  
  - **Measurements:** Distances, weights, and amounts of money are singular.
    
    - *Correct:* Ten pounds **is** a lot of money. (Not *are*)
  
  - **Titles:** Names of books or movies are singular.
  
  - **Separation:** If a clause separates subject and verb, ensure the verb agrees with the *actual* subject, not the intervening words.

### 2. Articles (A, An, The)

- **"Such":** Use *a/an* after "such" for countable nouns.
  
  - *Correct:* Such **a** wet summer. (Not *a such* wet summer).

- **Omission of "The":** Do not use *the* before names of meals (breakfast, lunch) or languages (English, French) when used generally.
  
  - *Correct:* Breakfast is at eight. (Not *The* breakfast).

### 3. Redundancy

- **Pronoun Redundancy:** Do not repeat the noun/pronoun when using a relative pronoun (*who, which, that*).
  
  - *Incorrect:* I found the book which I lost **it**.
  
  - *Correct:* I found the book which I lost.

- **Preposition Redundancy:** Avoid repeating prepositions if the relative pronoun already accounts for it.

### 4. Parts of Speech Confusion

- **Adjective vs. Adverb:** Linking verbs (*be, seem, become, feel, taste*) take an **Adjective**, not an adverb.
  
  - *Incorrect:* The milk turned **sourly**.
  
  - *Correct:* The milk turned **sour**.

### 5. Preposition Errors

- **Missing Preposition:** Some intransitive verbs need a preposition.
  
  - *Correct:* We listened **to** the music.

- **Extra Preposition:** Some transitive verbs (*answer, enter, approach, attack*) do **not** take a preposition.
  
  - *Incorrect:* We entered **into** the room.
  
  - *Correct:* We entered the room.

### 6. Lexical Errors (Word Choice)

- **Refuse vs. Deny:**
  
  - *Refuse:* To decline an offer/action.
  
  - *Deny:* To say something is untrue.

- **Damage vs. Damages:**
  
  - *Damage:* Destruction/Injury (Singular).
  
  - *Damages:* Money paid as compensation (Plural).

- **No Sooner:** Must be followed by **than**, not *when*.
  
  - *Correct:* No sooner had started **than** it rained.

## 2.4 Vocabulary Building

Expanding vocabulary involves learning nuances, opposites, and concise substitutions.

### A. Synonyms

Words with similar meanings.

- **Principle:** Synonyms must match the Part of Speech of the original word.

- **Examples:**
  
  - *Noun:* Behavior $\rightarrow$ Conduct
  
  - *Adjective:* Accurate $\rightarrow$ Exact; Strong $\rightarrow$ Powerful
  
  - *Verb:* Abandon $\rightarrow$ Leave; Confess $\rightarrow$ Admit
  
  - *Adverb:* Formerly $\rightarrow$ Previously

### B. Antonyms

Words with opposite meanings. Can be formed by:

1. **Adding Prefix:** *Honest* $\rightarrow$ *Dishonest*

2. **Changing Prefix:** *Import* $\rightarrow$ *Export*

3. **Changing Suffix:** *Harmful* $\rightarrow$ *Harmless*

4. **New Word:** *War* $\rightarrow$ *Peace*

### C. One Word Substitution

Replacing a phrase with a single, precise word.

| Phrase / Definition                                 | One Word Substitution |
| --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| The science of the structure of the human body      | **Anatomy**           |
| One who is present everywhere                       | **Omnipresent**       |
| One who is all-powerful                             | **Omnipotent**        |
| One who acts against religion                       | **Heretic**           |
| A feeling of having already experienced the present | **Déjà Vu**           |
| In exactly the same words as used originally        | **Verbatim**          |
| A person skilled in dictionary writing              | **Lexicographer**     |
| A place where money is coined                       | **Mint**              |
| A list of errors                                    | **Errata**            |
| A person/thing appointed for a specific purpose     | **Ad Hoc**            |
